\subsection{Interpretation Visibility Without Treating Reasons as Ground Truth}

\textbf{[Insert evidence about use or non-use of Acting Interpretation by Phrase.]}
The read-only reasons in the prototype are intended to make a proposed acting interpretation legible enough to accept or contest; they are not explanations of model internals or evidence that a suggestion is correct. If participants consult reasons mainly before large revisions, discuss them as \emph{interpretation visibility}. If participants ignore them, treat that as equally informative evidence that visual feedback or direct correction may be more useful than explanation during production.

This framing is particularly important for character acting because several interpretations may remain defensible. Any broader implication for creative AI should therefore concern negotiation among plausible interpretations rather than model confidence or prediction correctness.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

\textbf{[Update participant counts and any observed study-specific limitations.]}
Several limitations are already inherent to the present design. First, the study evaluates short dyadic scenes and a first-use workflow; it does not establish how the representation scales to long-form production, repeated professional use, overlapping speech, or scenes with more than two active characters. Second, execution is demonstrated through JALI and Maya, so the semantic representation may be more general than the specific control vocabulary and rig backend evaluated here. Third, the prototype exposes only a subset of acting decisions---visible affect, semantic attention, head behavior, and performative blink---and low-level Maya edits are not synchronized back into the Semantic Performance Tags or the canonical backend plan.

Fourth, the Study~A comparison holds camera-specific gaze calibration constant in order to isolate the authoring representation. The study therefore does not independently establish that manual gaze calibration improves gaze readability across camera viewpoints; that remains a system capability and a target for dedicated evaluation. Fifth, LLM outputs can vary across models, prompts, and software versions. Study~B should therefore be interpreted as evidence about the role of context within the tested generation setup, not as a claim that a particular model produces uniquely correct acting. Finally, acting interpretation is culturally, stylistically, and production-context dependent, and the current semantic vocabulary may not transfer unchanged across performance traditions.

Future work should examine multi-character coordination, alternative or branching semantic-performance proposals, explicit preservation constraints during regeneration, bidirectional synchronization between semantic decisions and low-level curve edits, longer-term professional use, and dedicated evaluation of camera-aware gaze calibration.
