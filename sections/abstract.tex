AI-assisted animation systems can generate and refine motion, but conversational performance also depends on interpretable acting decisions. We present a context-aware authoring workflow that exposes these decisions as editable \textit{Semantic Performance Tags} between narrative interpretation and deterministic animation execution. For dyadic dialogue, a pretrained LLM proposes shared initial states and sparse dialogue-anchored performance beats; animators can revise affect, attention, head, and blink decisions in Maya before compilation to JALI controls. We evaluate the workflow with ten usable participant records. Editable tags yielded higher descriptive final-performance ratings and revision clarity, but no workflow or workload comparison remained significant after Holm correction. In a text-only context ablation, Full-Context proposals significantly improved overall scene fit over Dialogue-Only proposals (Holm-adjusted $p=.039$), while individual intention, affect, gaze, and timing dimensions did not. The results support contextual interpretation and editable semantic representation as complementary layers for conversational character-animation authoring.
