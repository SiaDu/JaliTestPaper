AI-assisted animation systems increasingly support generation and low-level refinement, yet conversational character performance also depends on interpretive decisions about what each character is doing, attending to, and expressing over time. We present a context-aware authoring workflow that exposes these decisions as editable \textit{Semantic Performance Tags} between narrative interpretation and animation execution. For dyadic dialogue, a pretrained large language model proposes an initial performance state for both characters and sparse dialogue-anchored semantic beats; the Maya interface projects the resulting canonical plan into two character-specific tag panels where animators can inspect and revise acting-related affect, attention, head, and blink decisions before deterministic compilation to JALI and Maya controls. We evaluate the approach in two complementary studies with ten usable participant records: a within-subject authoring study comparing Direct Generation with Editable Semantic Performance Tags, and a text-only context ablation comparing Dialogue-Only with Full-Context proposals. Editable tags produced numerically higher final-performance ratings and the largest directional workflow difference in revision clarity, but no workflow or workload comparison remained significant after Holm correction. In the context ablation, Full-Context proposals were rated significantly higher in overall scene fit (Holm-adjusted $p=.039$), while individual intention, affect, gaze, and timing dimensions did not show corrected significant differences. These results suggest that additional context contributes most clearly to the holistic coherence of a proposed performance, while editable semantic tags provide a viable inspection and revision surface without yet demonstrating a general advantage in workload or final-performance quality.
